Design Process:
Step1: Training (Digital Phase): The network is designed virtually on a digital
computer. Input images are fed through a optical wave propagation model
(Huygens–Fresnel principle). Using standard deep learning algorithms
(backpropagation and gradient descent), the computer optimizes the
phase/amplitude shift required at every pixel on each layer.

Step3: Inference (Optical Execution): Light passes through the object (e.g., a

from layer to layer, the structured phase shifts constructively and destructively
interfere, directing the light energy to focus on a specific target coordinate on the
final detector plane corresponding to the correct class.

Key Advantages over Electronic Neural Networks
•Speed-of-Light Processing Latency: Inference completes as fast as light waves travel
through the physical layers of the network (typically nanoseconds or picoseconds).
Electronic processors (GPUs/TPUs) are bottlenecked by clock frequencies and electronic
switching speeds.
•Near-Zero Power Consumption: Passive diffractive optical layers consume zero
electrical power during inference. Power is only required for the light source (e.g.,
laser/LED) and the sensor photodetector array.
•Direct Processing of Native Optical Data: D2NNs directly compute on light, optical
signals, or visual imagery before it hits a sensor. This eliminates the need for power-
intensive analog-to-digital converters (ADCs) or image sensors prior to processing.
